summary: Superpixels and dual-contrast center-surround saliency are combined with
  optical flow and perceptual hashing to extract key-frame summaries from videos.
abstract: 'How to efficiently extract video content, i.e., video summarization, has
  long been a research hotspot in the field of computer vision. Simply detecting video
  summaries through image features such as color and texture can no longer obtain
  video summaries effectively and completely. Based on the visual attention pyramid
  model, an improved center-surround video summarization method with variable ratios
  and dual contrast computation is proposed. First, the video image sequence is partitioned
  into pixel blocks using the superpixel method to accelerate image computation. Then,
  the contrast feature differences of images under different color backgrounds are
  detected and fused. Finally, optical flow motion information is incorporated to
  merge the saliency results of static and dynamic images for extracting video key
  frames; during key frame extraction, a perceptual hash function is used for similarity
  judgment to complete video summary generation. Simulation experiments are conducted
  on the Segtrack V2, ViSal, and OVP datasets, and the results show that the proposed
  method can effectively extract regions of interest in images and obtain video summaries
  represented by key frame image sequences.
