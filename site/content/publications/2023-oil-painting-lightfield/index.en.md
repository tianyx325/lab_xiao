abstract: '**Purpose**　One of the important characteristics distinguishing oil paintings
  from other painting forms is the thickness variation of their paint layers. Therefore,
  oil painting similarity identification should consider not only the texture and
  color details of the painting but also the differences in surface paint thickness.
  To capture subtle variations in paint thickness, an oil painting similarity identification
  method based on surface light field feature point distributions is proposed. Light
  field imaging is employed to observe the imaging differences at different viewing
  angles caused by the surface thickness undulation of an oil painting, so as to quantitatively
  compute the similarity of the surface light field of the painting.


  **Methods**　A microlens array plate is used to encode the angular-domain variations
  of the oil painting surface, and the encoded surface light field is captured with
  a light field camera. On this basis, the set of feature points with large angular-domain
  differences in the surface light field is selected, and a polygonal vectorized description
  of the 2D distribution of this feature point set is obtained using the K-Means method.
  Furthermore, the Euclidean distances of the lines connecting key points and the
  included angles between line segments are computed to measure the similarity of
  the surface light field feature point distribution polygons.


  **Results**　The surface light field data of multiple real oil paintings are captured
  with an Illum light field camera, and the experimental results show that the proposed
  method can identify the similarity of oil paintings with subtle differences in paint
  thickness. In terms of the discrimination and detection accuracy for oil painting
  surface light fields, the proposed method significantly outperforms existing image
  feature matching identification methods.


  **Conclusions**　Experimental analysis shows that, compared with the classical intersection-over-union
  and similarity coefficients, the proposed oil painting similarity measure achieves
  higher measurement accuracy. Repeated experiments with adjusted distances between
  the encoding plate and the painting surface verify that the proposed method can
  effectively detect surface light field differences caused by thickness variations
  of 0.5 mm and above.
