abstract: 'Driven by the demand for intelligent robots to replace humans in performing
  extensive tasks such as exploration and epidemic prevention in various complex environments,
  scene recognition has attracted widespread attention from researchers. The purpose
  of scene recognition is to obtain high-level semantic information of a scene by
  extracting and analyzing its features, so as to infer the specific location; it
  is the foundation of simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) systems, autonomous
  driving, robot navigation, and loop closure detection. The rapid development of
  3D scanning technology enables people to quickly acquire point cloud data of various
  scenes with different scanners. Regardless of changes in scanning time and illumination
  conditions, the geometric information acquired from point cloud scenes exhibits
  good invariance; therefore, point-cloud-based scene recognition has become a research
  hotspot in the field of computer vision. This paper first summarizes recent scene
  recognition methods for point cloud data. Then, large-scale indoor/outdoor scene
  datasets used for scene recognition and the evaluation metrics for algorithm assessment
  are introduced, and the recognition rates of various algorithms are summarized.
  Finally, the problems and challenges in point-cloud-based scene recognition are
  pointed out, and future research trends are envisioned. The results help scholars
  in related fields quickly and comprehensively understand the current research status
  of point-cloud-based scene recognition and lay a foundation for further improving
  scene recognition accuracy.
