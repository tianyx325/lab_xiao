abstract: '**Purpose**　The open field test (OFT) is a commonly used experimental method
  in behavioral and pharmacological studies. To compare the behavioral differences
  between the test group and the reference group of mice, considerable effort is usually
  required to process and observe open field test data. Since open field test data
  are large in volume and heavily dependent on the subjective judgment of observers,
  the observation of behavioral differences in mice suffers from low precision and
  lacks quantitative evaluation metrics. To address these issues, this paper proposes
  a convolutional neural network (CNN)-based method for classifying open field test
  videos, which can automatically classify the behavioral differences between two
  groups of mice based on quantitative features.


  **Methods**　A 22-dimensional set of mouse movement behavior features is extracted
  from the spatial and temporal domains of the videos and quantified to generate feature
  matrices. Learnable behavioral feature matrix samples are then constructed via matrix
  splicing, and convolutional neural networks with different architectures are trained
  to classify the extracted behavioral feature matrix samples. The influence of network
  architecture on the classification results is analyzed. On the basis of classifying
  the two groups of mice, the importance of different dimensions of mouse behavioral
  features to classification accuracy is evaluated.


  **Results**　Experimental analysis on a real open field test dataset shows that the
  classification accuracy of the proposed algorithm reaches 99.25%. Moreover, the
  results reveal that the frequency of large-angle turning, the stayed regions, and
  the dwell time of mice contribute more to classification than other feature dimensions.


  **Conclusions**　The proposed spliced feature matrix learning method can accurately
  identify the differences between the open field test videos of two groups of mice,
  and its classification accuracy significantly outperforms existing manual analysis
  and classical machine learning methods.
